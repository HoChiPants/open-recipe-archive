# Recipe content policy

This project is built for sharing useful cooking data, but “recipes cannot be copyrighted” is too broad to use as a contribution rule.

In the United States, a mere ingredient list and a simple procedure generally are not copyrightable. Creative explanations, expressive directions, photographs, illustrations, and a cookbook's original selection or arrangement may still be protected. The U.S. Copyright Office explains this distinction in [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf) and its [copyright FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html).

Trademark is a different issue. Names and branding may identify the source of goods or services even when a recipe method is not protected by copyright. The [USPTO trademark overview](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark) provides the basic distinction.

## What contributors may submit

- Recipes they created and describe in their own words.
- Traditional or common recipes written as fresh, concise functional directions.
- Adaptations that use original wording and identify the source or inspiration.
- Factual nutrition or seasonality data from a source the contributor is permitted to use.

## What contributors should not submit

- Text copied or lightly paraphrased from a cookbook, paid publication, or food blog.
- Photographs or illustrations without clear permission and compatible licensing.
- A proprietary collection scraped wholesale from another service.
- Brand presentation that implies sponsorship or affiliation.
- Private, confidential, or trade-secret material.

Each contributor is responsible for having the right to submit their contribution and to dedicate repository data under CC0. Maintainers may remove disputed material while a claim is reviewed. This policy is practical project guidance, not legal advice, and laws vary by jurisdiction.
